    Gable, Hip or Sundeck?

    Choosing a roof style and layout for your boat dock

    Home › Dock Planning Blog › Choosing a Roof Style and Layout

    The roof style you choose shapes how your dock looks from the shore. It also decides how much shade your boat gets and whether you gain an extra level of living space on the water. Here’s a walk through the Flotation Systems aluminum dock collection to help you choose the style that fits the way you use your waterfront.

    Start with how you’ll use the dock

    Before you pick a roof, think about your days on the water. Do you mostly need a place to tie up the boat and jump in? Do you want shade and protection for your boat? Or do you picture an upper deck for sunsets, grilling and gathering with family? Your answer narrows down the style quickly.

    Dock piers and platforms

    Piers and platforms are the simplest starting point: an open aluminum walkway and deck without a roof. They’re ideal for swimming, fishing, launching kayaks and tying up. A platform also makes a great base you can expand later.

    Gable roof docks

    A gable roof has the classic peaked shape, with two sloped sides meeting at a ridge. It gives a traditional, boathouse look and good headroom over the slip, and it sheds rain and debris down both sides.

    Hip roof docks

    A hip roof slopes down on all four sides. It has a lower, more streamlined profile than a gable roof, which many owners like on open water. The sloped sides also give even coverage around the slip.

    Sundeck docks

    A sundeck dock trades the pitched roof for a flat upper deck, so the top of the dock becomes usable space. You get shade for your boat below and a place to relax above, with views across the water.

    Sundeck combo docks

    A sundeck combo pairs an upper sundeck with a covered roof section. You get open-air space in the sun and a shaded spot on the same dock. It’s the most complete setup for owners who spend whole days on the water.

    Choosing your layout

    Once you have a style in mind, the layout decides how it all fits together. Things to plan:

    • Number of slips. One boat today may become a boat and personal watercraft later. Plan for the fleet you expect to have.
    • Walkway length. How far the dock needs to reach depends on how quickly your shoreline drops off to usable depth.
    • Platform space. Extra deck area gives room for chairs, storage and getting gear on and off the boat.

    See the slip configurations page for layout options to talk through with your dealer.

    Make it yours: color and design collections

    The frame and accents can match your home and shoreline. The Designer Color Series offers nine nature-inspired finishes: Cedar Creek, Charleston, Driftwood, Savannah, Silver Ridge, Woods N’ Water, Timberline, Greystone and Black Stone.

    For even more character, two signature collections add architectural details:

    • The Beaumont Series brings Southern style with arched supports and curved balusters.
    • The Lodge Collection adds rustic crow’s feet bracing inspired by mountain lodges.

    Don’t forget the deck under a sundeck

    If you choose a sundeck or sundeck combo, consider LockDry® watertight aluminum decking up top. It keeps rain from dripping through, so the area below stays dry for your boat, storage or a shaded hangout.
