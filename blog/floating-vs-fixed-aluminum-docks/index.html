    Floating vs. Fixed Aluminum Docks

    How to choose the right dock for your lake

    Home › Dock Planning Blog › Floating vs. Fixed Aluminum Docks

    The first big decision in planning a new boat dock isn’t the roof style or the color. It’s whether your dock should float or stand on a fixed frame. Get it right and your dock will be easy to use in every season. Get it wrong and you may face a deck that sits too high, too low or takes a beating from the water.

    Flotation Systems builds aluminum boat docks in both floating and fixed designs. Here’s how to tell which one fits your waterfront.

    How a floating dock works

    A floating dock rests on flotation and is anchored in place, so it rises and falls with the water. The deck stays at the same height above the surface whether your lake is at full pool or several feet down. That makes boarding, tying up and stepping off the boat feel the same all year.

    How a fixed (stationary) dock works

    A fixed dock, also called a stationary dock, stands on legs or pilings set into the lake or river bed. The deck stays put no matter what the water does. Fixed docks feel solid and still underfoot, and they are a natural fit where the water level stays fairly steady.

    4 questions to help you decide

    1. How much does your water level change? This is the most important question. On reservoirs and lakes with big seasonal swings, a fixed dock can end up towering over the water or sitting under it. A floating dock solves that by moving with the water. If your level barely changes, a fixed dock is a strong option.

    2. How deep is the water where the dock will sit? Deep water favors floating docks, because long legs or pilings get harder to set the deeper you go. Shallow to moderate depths suit fixed docks well.

    3. How much wave and wake action do you get? Busy lakes mean constant boat wakes, and open water means wind-driven waves. A floating dock moves with the water, while a fixed dock stays rigid. On high-traffic lakes, stability engineering matters. Flotation Systems’ WAVEPro™ Technology (Wave Attentive Versatility Engineered) was developed to keep docks stable in heavy wave action and extreme weather.

    4. What do you want to build on top? Think about the full dock you have in mind: a simple pier or platform, a gable or hip roof over your boat slips, or a sundeck combo with space for lounging up top. Your dealer can tell you which styles work with the system that fits your water.

    Quick comparison

    Floating dockFixed (stationary) dock
    Moves with water levelYesNo
    Best water conditionsChanging levels, deeper waterSteady levels, shallow to moderate depth
    Feel underfootGentle movementSolid and still
    Held in place byAnchoring systemLegs or pilings in the lake bed

    Why aluminum for either choice

    Whichever system you pick, an aluminum frame won’t rot, warp or splinter like wood can after years in the water. Pair it with LockDry® watertight aluminum decking, and you get a dock built for less upkeep and more time on the water.

    Get a recommendation for your shoreline

    Every waterfront is different. Your local Flotation Systems dealer can look at your water depth, level changes and bottom conditions and recommend the right system for you. You can also compare both systems side by side in the Floating vs. Stationary Docks section on our homepage.
